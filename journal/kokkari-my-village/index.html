Kokkari, the village we call home

A host's guide to Kokkari — the morning swim, where we send guests to eat, and why the real luxury here is calm, quiet and nobody knocking on the door.

Updated 2026-11-11 · facts checked 2026-10-31 · translation pending (Greek)

Short answer: we live in Kokkari — one of our hosts grew up in the village and the family lives here year-round — and when guests ask what to do here our answer is always the same: swim, eat, walk, and let the village set the pace. Here is how we show people our home.

We are biased, happily: we think Samos is the best island in the Aegean, and Kokkari the best part of Samos. You should still take our recommendations with that in mind — but they are honest ones, because they are the same ones we give our family.

The morning swim

Kokkari's beach is about 900 metres of pebble right in front of the village, and the water gets deep quickly. The secret nobody tells you: go early. Before the north wind builds, the bay is glass, the tavernas are still wiping their tables, and the swimming is all yours. Shoes you can swim in — please, we mention this every time, because pebbles. Two minutes from our door, and no plan required.

For a longer morning, walk west. Lemonakia, twenty minutes on foot, has the gentlest entry and pines that smell like summer. Tsamadou, another five minutes on, is the big one — clear, cold, deep, and calm even when the wind is up. Both are on the coast road; the walk is a road walk, so wear something visible and face the traffic. That is all three beaches within two kilometres of our door, and the reason we never need to go further for a swim.

Where we send guests to eat

The waterfront is a row of tavernas on the water — the kind of places where the fish came off the boat that morning and the octopus hangs drying in the sun. Our rule for guests: pick the one that smells right, or ask us and we will tell you where we ate last night.

Up the lane, behind the beach, is the village bakery — go before ten for the bougatsa, a family favourite and therefore beyond argument.

And when guests ask for one place they must not miss, we tell them to take a table anywhere on the water as the sun drops behind the headland and order whatever the waiter is proud of. It has never once been wrong.

What "wellness" means here

People come to Samos and expect the island to hand them a spa. It will not. What it will hand you — and what we believe is worth more — is this: a room that is clean and calm, a village where the loudest thing at night is the sea, mornings that belong to you, and no programme to keep. Wellness here is not a treatment list. It is quiet, order, salt water, and nobody needing anything from you. We keep the apartments simple on purpose, because the calm of the apartment is the first room of the holiday.

Privacy is the real luxury

Our four homes are individual — each with its own door, its own key, its own rhythm. No reception, no lobby, no shared corridor where you have to be sociable. We are here when you need us and invisible when you do not. We have learned that the rarest thing a holiday can offer is the feeling that nobody is managing you. That is what we try to build: the place runs itself, so you can run your own days.

The questions guests ask us (and what we did)

We keep a list of the questions guests ask most. It is the most useful book we keep. So far: is the beach really pebble? Do we need a car? How many stairs are there? — all answered now in the guides we wrote from those questions, the beach one first (read it here). If you arrive and the guide does not answer something, tell us — the next version of it will. That is a promise, not a slogan.

Kokkari is not a secret and we will not pretend it is one. It is a working village on a beautiful bay, and our family was lucky enough to make it home. If you come, we will point you at the water and tell you about the bakery. The rest, happily, is up to you.
